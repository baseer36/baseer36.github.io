Rug Size Guide: what size rug for every room

Nine out of ten rugs we see in UAE homes are one size too small. Here is how to get it right the first time, with numbers rather than guesswork.

9 min read·Naqsh Carpets

  • Dining room rug sizes

  • Hallways and corridors

A rug that is too small does something specific and unfortunate: it makes the furniture look like it is drifting apart, and it makes the floor look bigger than the room. A rug that is correctly sized does the opposite — it gathers the furniture into one group and the room reads as deliberate.

The reason people buy small is price. A 160 × 230 cm rug costs roughly half what a 250 × 350 cm costs in the same quality, and in a showroom the smaller one looks generous. On a floor it rarely is. If budget is the constraint, it is almost always better to buy a larger rug in a cheaper construction than a small rug in an expensive one.

The one rule that matters: front legs on

In a seating area, every chair and sofa should have at least its front legs standing on the rug. That single rule solves most layout problems. It ties the furniture to the rug, it stops the rug reading as a floating mat, and it gives you a visual boundary for the room.

There are three accepted layouts, and they differ in how much of the furniture sits on the rug:

  • All legs on — the most generous. Every piece sits fully on the rug, with 20–40 cm of rug showing beyond the furniture. Needs a large room and a large rug.
  • Front legs on — the standard, and the one we recommend for most UAE apartments. The front legs of every seat rest on the rug; the back legs are on the floor.
  • All legs off — only acceptable for a small accent rug under a coffee table, and only in a small room. It is the layout most often chosen by accident.

Living room rug sizes

Measure your seating group — not the room. Put a tape measure across the outside edges of the sofa and the chairs facing it, then add 40 cm at each end. That is your minimum rug width.

Room / seatingMinimum sizeComfortable size
Small apartment lounge, 2-seat sofa + 1 chair160 × 230 cm200 × 300 cm
Standard apartment living room, 3-seat sofa + 2 chairs200 × 300 cm250 × 350 cm
Villa living room or open-plan seating250 × 350 cm300 × 400 cm
Large villa / double seating group300 × 400 cmTwo rugs, or custom
The most common mistakeBuying 160 × 230 cm for a standard three-seat living room. The sofa is usually 210–230 cm wide on its own, so the rug ends exactly where the sofa ends and the whole group looks pinched. In that room the answer is 200 × 300 cm at the very least.

Dining room rug sizes

This one is pure arithmetic, and it is the easiest size in the house to get right. A dining chair needs roughly 70 cm of travel to pull out and sit down. If the back legs drop off the edge of the rug mid-movement, the chair tips and the rug wears at that line.

Table length + 140 cm, by table width + 140 cm. That is the whole formula.

Table seatsTypical tableRug size
4120 × 80 cm260 × 220 cm → use 250 × 350 cm
6180 × 90 cm320 × 230 cm → use 300 × 400 cm

Bedroom rug sizes

Bedrooms are judged with bare feet in the first ten seconds of the day, which is the only test that matters. Two layouts work:

  1. One large rug under the bed. It starts just below the bedside tables and runs under the bottom two-thirds of the bed, extending 60–75 cm beyond each side. For a king bed that means 250 × 350 cm; for a queen, 200 × 300 cm.
  2. A pair of runners. One 80 × 300 cm runner down each side of the bed. This costs far less, it is easier to clean, and it puts wool exactly where your feet land.

Hallways and corridors

Leave 10–20 cm of floor showing on each side of a runner — a runner that reaches wall to wall looks like failed fitted carpet. Standard runners are 80 × 300 cm. If your corridor is longer, have it cut and bound to length from a single roll so the pattern runs continuously rather than meeting at a join.

Keep the pile low. A door needs about 15 mm of clearance, and a deep pile in a doorway wears in a visible line within a year.

Majlis carpets are measured differently

A majlis has seating around the perimeter and an open centre, which inverts the usual logic. You are not sizing a rug to a furniture group; you are usually covering most of the floor. Measure the full room, subtract 20–30 cm at each wall, and expect to need a custom size — standard rectangles rarely fit a majlis well.

Pile depth matters more here than anywhere else in the house, because people sit on the floor. Anything under 12 mm stops being comfortable after about twenty minutes.

A note on cm and feet

The Gulf trade works in centimetres, the American trade works in feet. The common conversions, rounded the way the trade rounds them:
